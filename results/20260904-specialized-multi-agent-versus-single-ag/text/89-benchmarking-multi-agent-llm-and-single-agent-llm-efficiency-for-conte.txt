LLM Efficiency for Contextual Text Generation

Abstract-Text generation is commonly done with large 
language models, yet the question of whether the task breaking 
down into several agents is better-performing than the classic 
single-agent model remains open. To examine this the focus of 
this study was to compare the performance of Multi agent LLM 
and Single agent LLM for contextual text generation. The aim 
was to determine which system generates the answers that are 
nearer to the reference text and reflect the valuable information 
more effectively. This was achieved by comparing both setups 
using Google's Gemini-Flash-Latest model. For standard 
benchmarking fundamental science domains were selected as 
the basis of each setup. The Single agent setup worked like a 
traditional monolithic LLM. It independently generated 
responses for each query. On the other hand, the Multi agent 
setup divided the given tasks into subproblem and solved them 
in a structured way. It had multiple agents each doing their 
specific separate task like there were distinct agents for 
retrieval, reasoning, and summarization. To evaluate the 
generated outputs of each system they were tested on NLP 
metrics like BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, and BERTScore under 
same conditions. These metrics were selected because of their 
ability to analyze text similarity, fluency, and semantical 
accuracy. The results showed that a multi agent system 
outperforms a single agent system. 
Keywords-Large Language Models, Single Agent Multi 
Agent, RAG 
I. INTRODUCTION 
The field of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Leaming 
has been expanding at a very fast pace especially in the terms 
of the model complexity which poses a significant challenge 
for both expert and non-expert audiences. After the 
breakthrough of Neural Networks in early 2010s, the 
trajectory was further accelerated by the introduction of 
Transformer Architecture, [I] which in tum enabled the 
development of Large Language Models such as BERT, GPT, 
Gemini, Mistral etc. 
The Large Language Models have noteworthy capabilities 
in generation, reasoning and multimodal integration. These 
capabilities can be integrated into existing and upcoming 
software with the help of certain frameworks such as Agentic 
Systems. The Agentic Systems can be either a conventional 
single-agent system or an advanced multi-agent system [2]. 
Conventional systems perform better at doing a single task at 
a time whereas, the multi-agent system has multiple agents 
performing their specific tasks and working simultaneously to 
provide better results. The conventional systems suffer from 
issues such as hallucinations, lack of factual grounding and 
reduced interpretability, which may create a research gap in 
robust, collaborative and modular frameworks. In multi-agent 
systems we divide the tasks into smaller and role specific 
components for instance retrieval, reasoning and 
summarization can be considered agent-specific tasks. The

specialized agents work together in order to provide improved 
accuracy, contextual reasoning and concise knowledge 
delivery, thereby enabling specialized collaboration. 
The aim was to conduct a structural comparison between 
single-agent and multi-agent systems. It was done to check 
their effectiveness in processing and delivery of the required 
output. The evaluation is done by leveraging similarity scores 
such as BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR etc. The findings will 
therefore not only depict the architectural distinction between 
the two systems but also the accuracy, contextual grounding 
and interpretability of the Agentic Systems. 
II. LITERATURE REVIEW 
Rapid advancements in the field of Artificial Intelligence 
are due to contributions of Large Language Models. Vaswani 
et al. introduced the foundational model of the Transformer 
Architecture [l]. This transformer model was further 
revolutionized, Devlin et al. introduced BERT which is 
essentially an Encoder based model, Brown et al. GPT-3 and 
OpenAI et al. further enhanced the generating capabilities of 
the transformer model by increasing the parameter scale and 
incorporated advanced alignment techniques such as 
reinforcement learning from human feedback in order to 
produce more context-aware responses [3] [4] [5]. Apart from 
the generalized gains, the language models have also inspired 
domain-specific applications, Bommasani et al. surveyed and 
highlighted the societal and technical impact of LLMs in 
healthcare, law and education domains. [6] 
To meet the limitations ofLLMs regarding factuality and 
retrieval of the context, RAG (Retrieval Augmented 
Generation) methods or single-agent systems have been 
formed. The proposal put forth in order to integrate a dense 
retrieval mechanism with a generative process to foster more 
grounding and factual correctness in open domain question-
answering, is a major breakthrough [7]. Azacard & Grave et 
al. wrote on Fusion-in-Decoder that expanded this idea to 
show how retrieval provides additional robustness and 
grounding to the predictions [8]. It was also noted that despite 
these advancements, single agent systems typically do not 
have represent additional robustness and grounding or scale 
up dominantly in complex work.flows. 
The challenges faced by single-agent systems were 
addressed by Multi-Agent Frameworks, Li et al. launched 
CAMEL which is essentially role-based agentic worktlow, 
engages in goal-driven dialogues for solving complex tasks 
[9]. A conversational multi-agent framework AutoGen, Wu et 
al. was proposed which enables the task decomposition and 
coordination. Some recent works have explored the 
decomposition of tasks into agent-specific roles like retrieval, 
planning, summarization etc [IO]. Some examples of such task 
based agentic systems are MAGI and MetaGPT. These

Ill. METHODOLOGY 
A. Overview 
The main goal of this study was to compare and 
benchmark single agent LLM with Multi agent LLM on 
similar set of tasks. The target result of this experiment was to 
look at how each system's output matched and aligned the 
reference text. It also focused on how well they conveyed the 
relevant information in their generated responses. To achieve 
this comparison three fundamental scientific topics were 
chosen as the base subjects. These topics consisted of 
photosynthesis, the water cycle and respiration. They were 
selected because of their factual relevance and because they 
were suitable to benchmark these systems for evaluation. 
The study used Google's "gemini-tlash-latest" model as 
the foundational/base model. This model was used to 
configure both sin le a ent s stem and multi le a ent s stem. 
Slart 
This model was selected for this setup because of Gemini's 
strong language understanding capabilities. The coherent and 
context aware answers given by this model were suitable for 
both systems. 
In the single agent setup as shown in "Fig. 1 ", the system 
was tasked to directly respond the input query without any 
intermediate reasoning It worked to mimic the style of any 
traditional monolithic LLM. It processes prompt and query 
directly without splitting the input it into smaller subproblems. 
To do this the setup first creates a list of topics along with 
questions for those topics. Each question is then sent to the 
model. The model then directly responds to the question. 
On the other hand, Multi agent system as shown in "Fig 2" 
divides the problem into parts. Each subproblem is solved by 
a separate agent that specializes in solving that problem. For 
this specific system the specialised agents are Retriever Agent, 
Reasoner Agent and Summarizer Agent. As the name suggests 
the retriever agent identifies and then extract the relevant 
information that is related to input query. The reasoner agent, 
reasons the retrieved content and creates a domain specific 
answer for the question. Finally, the summarizer agent 
summarizes the content with most relevant information and 
makes sure the final output that is human comprehendible. 
So, all these modular agents work together to 
formcollaborative team and give more precise outputs. 
Fairness was ensured by keeping all other conditions the 
same. The same topics, queries and source explanations were 
employed for both methods. Finally, to evaluate both these 
setups common NLP/ LLM evaluation techniques are used. 
These consists of BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, and 
BERTScore.

B. Dataset 
The dataset used for this comparative study was manually 
created. This dataset was small controlled, and it was focused 
on basic scientific topics. The main topics covered in this 
dataset were Photosynthesis, the water cycle, and respiration. 
For the structure of this dataset each topic was divided into 
elements. These elements were query, reference and 
explanation. The query represented the small question to be 
answered. The reference element acted as the standard for 
evaluation and the explanation basically was set of supporting 
documents. 
The dataset was set into the CSV-format in which every 
row corresponded to one query instance and its respective 
reference answer and explanation documents. This tabular 
format enabled the experimentation to be evaluated by 
loading, processing and accessing supporting documents 
easily. 
The single agent system directly responded to queries 
without accessing the documents. On the other hand, multi 
agent setup retrieved, reasoned and summarized the query. 
Despite the dataset being simple and small it's served its 
purpose. It allowed testing of models in a properly controlled 
and benchmarkable space. 
C. Evaluation Metrics 
To evaluate each setup four well known NLP metrics were 
used: BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, and BERTScore. These 
metrics were chosen as they are popular in the text generation 
and summarization. BLEU which stands for Bilingual 
Evaluation Understudy is a metric that checks how much the 
generated text is similar to the reference text using n-grams 
overlapping technique. This metrics is useful in evaluating the 
fluency and semantic relevance of the text that is being 
generated [13]. However, BLEU mainly checks matching on 
743 
word level in the output text. It does not fully measure content 
coverage with respect to reference text. To check the content 
coverage ROUGE was used. 
ROUGE which stands for Recall-Oriented Understudy for 
Gisting Evaluation is a metrics that evaluate how much the 
reference content is getting retained in the output that is 
getting generated. It is calculated in its multiple variation. The 
ROUGE-I checked the unigram overlapping of the text, 
ROUGE-2 checked the bigram overlapping of the text, and 
ROUGE-L checked the longest-sequence overlaps of the text. 
Hence this metrics is good for judging the content preservation 
ofa model. [14] 
Another metric METEOR which stands for Metric for 
Evaluation of Translation with Explicit Ordering was also 
used. This metric works similar BLEU and ROUGE but 
provides better and deeper analysis. It compares the generated 
text and reference based on meaning, word forms, and order. 
This metrics takes into account synonyms and small wording 
changes making it more reliable. [ 15] 
To compare meaning of sentences BERTScore metric was 
used. It uses advanced language models like BERT to make 
its analysis. It basically judges that generated text has same 
meaning as the reference text. [16]

agent system achieved 0.5867, as compared to 0. I 748 for the 
single-agent model. 
Overall, these results show that using multiple agents 
improves both the quality and accuracy ofresponses.

VI. CONCLUSION 
The study successfully compared single agent model with 
multi agent model. The results showed that multi agent model 
performed better and gave more relevant answers as compared 
to a single agent model. This shows that dividing the problem 
into smaller parts and then solving it gives comparatively 
better results. Each agent is well versed with their work and 
can provide a more to the point and accurate answer to the 
problem. This property is useful in complex problem. In 
complex problem the probability of hallucination increases in 
monolithic LLM. They tend to get confused when multiple 
tasks are assigned and give result that is not accurate. Whereas 
dividing the problem in modular parts allows each part to be 
processed individually. This guarantees a more reliable result 
as observed in the case of multi agent LLM.

These findings show that system design is important in 
LLM-based systems. The performance and the result of an 
LLM framework does not depend only on its size or training 
data. It also depends on how the framework is structured and 
how well the tasks are assigned. Dividing the main goal into 
smaller sub tasks can help these frameworks manage and 
solve complex problems more clearly and with less 
hallucination. Overall, it can also reduce errors in tasks with 
multiple requirements. This way of structuring can help build 
and develop more trustworthy and transparent LLM 
frameworks. It can also provide these frameworks with more 
modularity and automation power. It is especially useful in 
areas and domains like healthcare, legal work, education, and 
research. 
Overall, the experiment showed that dividing complex 
tasks into smaller roles gives better results and higher-quality 
output for the required query. 
VII. FUTURE SCOPE 
The future growth and opportunities of this study are 
centred around expanding the use of multi-agent system. It 
aims to solve more complex and challenging problems to give 
more reliable result. Real world tasks cannot be as simple as 
one step problems, they are dense network of interconnected 
sub-problems. They are usually made up of many interlinked 
problems. While solving such problem that are 
interconnected, monolithic LLMs becomes more susceptible 
to increased hallucination probability in their responses. This 
study proves that multi agent performs better and have 
potential to solve real world problems with better accuracy as 
compared to monolithic LLM. 
Another important direction is to apply the multi-agent 
framework in problems that require very close alignment with 
reference texts and the output is highly dependent on it. This 
is seen in applications like generating content for educational 
purposes or scientific research summarization or in legal or 
medical fields with sensitive data. These all tasks are highly 
affected by hallucination, and they require more precision and 
accurate result to justify the output. To provide what is needed 
multi agent systems have greater potential to grow in these 
fields as compared to single agent LLM.
