BATITONG: Deterministic Reliability for
LLM-Driven Offensive-Security Orchestration
Abd Rahman Wahid
Computer Club Oriented Network, Utility & Technology (COCONUT), Indonesia
abdrahman.wahid@coconut.or.id
Abstract.State-of-the-art LLM-driven offensive-security systems are evaluated on frontier,paidlanguage models,
which conflates two questions: how much reliability comes from the model, and how much can be supplied by
architecture? We presentBatitong, an open, single-container orchestration framework that runs an end-to-end
security assessment onzero-cost(free-tier) large language models (LLMs) and compensates for their weakness with
a deterministic reliability scaffold: an executor-independent evidence gate, a coverage oracle that blocks premature
finalisation, a Mixture-of-Agents (MoA) adjudication panel, adaptive per-plane model routing, a resilience watchdog
with model fallback, and an evidence-gated closed-loop self-improvement store, all behind a Model Context Protocol
(MCP) execution boundary. We formalise each mechanism, give its algorithm, and audit its presence in code
(46/46 checks). We then analyse a real deployment of 66 assessment campaigns over 20 de-identified targets (598
findings, 416 carrying both the pre-gate claim and post-gate result). Because both are stored, disabling the verifier
is measurable as acounterfactualwithout re-execution: the gate corrects 28/416 (6.7%) over-claimed findings (every
one a confirmed→suspected correction for missing reproducible evidence), while leaving the proof-of-concept
(PoC) count unchanged (90 in both views). Across exploitation targets, 27/68 (39.7%) reached a reproducible
PoC. We release the harness, a pre-registered ablation protocol, and an authorized benchmark. The contribution is
architectural and methodological: a reproducible composition that makes free-model offensive-security output more
trustworthy, measured rather than asserted.
Keywords:orchestrated offensive security; LLM agents; mixture-of-agents; evidence-gated verification; repro-
ducibility; zero-cost LLMs.
1 Introduction
Manual penetration testing is bounded by time, cost,
coverage consistency, and the supply of skilled testers.
LLM-driven agents promise to relax these bounds, and a
line of systems now performs reconnaissance, exploitation,
and reporting with limited human intervention [1, 2, 3,
5]. A property unites the strongest published results:
they run on frontier,paidmodels. Reliable LLM-driven
offensive security, to date, carries an API bill.
This paper asks a different question:if the model is
weak and free, how much reliability can architecture re-
cover?The question is practical (cost and reproducibility
for resource-constrained teams and educators) and scien-
tific (separating model strength from architectural scaf-
folding tells us where the reliability of an LLM security
assessment actually comes from). It also extends the au-
thor’s line ofdefensivedetection frameworks [33, 34, 35]
to theoffensiveside of the same threat landscape.
We presentBatitong, an open orchestration frame-
work that runs an end-to-end assessment on free-tier
LLMs. Its premise: a probabilistic executor (the LLM)
must be wrapped indeterministiccontrols that neither
trust the model’s self-assessment nor let it exceed its
authorization. The model never touches a tool directly;
every action passes an MCP boundary. Findings the
model claims are re-checked by an executor-independent,
deterministic gate before they enter a report. Borderline
decisions may be escalated to a panel of independent free
models. A coverage oracle refuses to close a campaign
while exploitation targets remain undriven. A watchdog
fails a dead or hung model over to the next. Verified out-
comes are distilled into an evidence-gated, self-pruning
knowledge store recalled on later runs.
We do not claim free models match frontier models.
We claim, and give initial evidence for, a narrower propo-
sition: a deterministic reliability scaffold measurably re-
duces over-claimed findings and preserves demonstrated
exploitation, delivered at zero API cost in one repro-
ducible container.

2 Related Work
LLM-driven penetration testing.PentestGPT intro-
duced a task-tree formulation, evaluated with a human
in the loop [1]. Fanget al.reported agents autonomously
compromising web applications and exploiting one-day
vulnerabilities, and a hierarchical multi-agent variant
(HPTSA) [2, 3, 4]. Open frameworks such as CAI pack-
age agentic tooling [5].Batitongshares the end-to-end
goal but is designed forfreemodels and foregrounds an
executor-independent verification layer.
Multi-model adjudication.Mixture-of-Agents
shows open models used as proposers with an aggrega-
tor can rival a stronger single model [9]. We apply this
narrowly, to adjudicate borderline security decisions on a
tool-free panel that cannot alter the campaign.
Self-improving agents.Voyager grows a skill li-
brary from experience [12]; Reflexion adds verbal self-
critique [13]. Our self-improvement isevidence-gated(a
lesson must trace to a confirmed/exploited finding), the
write path is deterministic (no model call), and stale
knowledge is rotated out.
Cost-aware inference.FrugalGPT studies cheap-
first cascades for general tasks [22]; we generalise the cost
framing to a full offensive pipeline on a free pool with
evidence-graded per-plane routing.
Foundations.The framework builds on established
LLM reasoning and agent techniques, including chain-of-
thought and tree-of-thought prompting, self-refinement,
and retrieval-augmented generation, with alignment from
AI feedback [18, 17, 14, 20, 21], and on the frontier models
that current autonomous systems rely on [29]. Prior LLM
penetration-testing studies and surveys inform its threat
framing [7, 6, 8], and its methodology and scoring follow
standard practice [30, 31, 32].

3 System Architecture
Batitongseparatesreasoningfromaction. The LLM
orchestrator plans and decides but never invokes a tool;
every action is issued through an MCP server that medi-
ates and constrains execution inside an isolated container
image bundling standard offensive tooling (Fig. 1). This
boundary is the structural precondition for the reliability
scaffold: because all effects pass one mediated interface,
they can be logged, constrained by rules of engagement
(RoE), and re-examined.
Functional-role pipeline.Technology expertise is
swappable knowledge; the functional roles are constant
(Fig. 2). A read-onlyplannerbuilds an attack tree with
a coverage stop-condition; a mode-selectedorchestrator
routes sub-tasks under an RoE ceiling;specialistsengineer
payloads and run tools via MCP; an independentverifier
re-examines each claimed finding; acuratordistils lessons;
a tool-freeoraclepowers the MoA panel. No agent grades
its own work.

9 Discussion
The clearest supported statement concerns the veri-
fier: on real deployment data, a deterministic, executor-
independent gate corrected 6.7% (28/416) of over-claimed
findings and removed no demonstrated exploit. This is
the mechanism central to the thesis (that trustworthiness
can come from architecture) and the one for which a clean
counterfactual exists, because both the pre-gate claim
and post-gate result are stored. The correction is mod-
est and specific: it withholds unsupported confirmations,
precisely the behaviour a security report needs.
Weak models over-claim and stall more often than
strong ones; the scaffold addresses both structurally (gate
and coverage oracle constrain over-claiming and prema-
ture closure; watchdog and routing address instability;
the panel supplies a second opinion where a single weak
model is least reliable). None of this requires a stronger
model, which is what makes zero-cost operation feasible.
Our mechanisms have antecedents; the contribution is
their composition under a zero-cost constraint with a
deterministic verifier at the centre, and an open harness
that lets the composition be measured. For resource-
constrained teams and education, a free single-container

12 Conclusion
A substantial part of the machinery for a trustworthy
LLM-driven offensive-security assessment can be supplied
by architecture rather than by an expensive model. On a
real deployment, an executor-independent deterministic
verifier corrected 6.7% of over-claimed findings while pre-
serving every demonstrated exploit, at zero inference cost,
in a single reproducible container. We present this as an
architectural and methodological contribution with an
honest observational evidence base and a pre-registered
path to controlled results. The reframing we advocate
is modest but useful: the interesting question for LLM-
driven offensive security on commodity models is not
whether the model is strong, but whether the system
around it is disciplined.
