Enhancing declarative business process 
management availability through generative AI
Wesley da Silva Santos1*, Juliana Rezende Coutinho2, Fernanda Baião2, Georges Miranda Spyrides1 and 
Hélio Côrtes Vieira Lopes1
Introduction
Organizations employ Business Process Management (BPM) as a comprehensive meth -
odology to enhance their operational effectiveness (Dumas et al. 2018). This discipline 
encompasses a range of activities including process design, implementation, monitor -
ing, analysis, and continuous improvement. Central to BPM practice is the development 
and maintenance of process models - formal representations that document how work 
should flow through an organization. These models play a vital role in enabling stake -
holders to visualize, evaluate, and optimize their business operations.
Abstract
Business process models serve as crucial artifacts in modern organizations, enabling 
stakeholders to understand, analyze, and improve their operational workflows. 
While traditional imperative process models are well-represented in public 
repositories, declarative models—particularly those capturing multiple process 
perspectives—remain scarce due to privacy concerns and the proprietary nature 
of business operations. This scarcity significantly hampers research, education, 
and innovation in declarative process management. We address this challenge 
by introducing Terpsichora, a framework that leverages large language models to 
generate synthetic multi-perspective declarative (MP-Declare) process models. Our 
strategy combines input formulation and constrained generation techniques with 
automated validation mechanisms to ensure the generation of diverse, realistic 
process models while maintaining structural validity and semantic coherence. 
Through comprehensive evaluation of 2000 synthetic models across two generation 
approaches, we demonstrate that our framework successfully generates models with 
diverse complexity metrics and high labeling convention compliance. The generated 
models effectively support various analytical tasks, including process mining and 
conformance checking, while preserving essential business domain characteristics. 
This research establishes a novel pathway for addressing the availability challenge 
in declarative process modeling, enabling researchers and practitioners to access 
diverse, high-quality process models.

The landscape of process modeling encompasses two fundamental paradigms (Der 
Aalst et al. 2009). Traditional imperative approaches prescribe rigid, step-by-step work -
flows that must be followed precisely, while declarative frameworks establish boundaries 
through rules and restrictions that guide acceptable process behaviors. The declarative 
paradigm, exemplified by languages like Declare, demonstrates particular value in con -
temporary business environments that demand operational agility. By emphasizing the 
underlying principles and limitations that shape process execution rather than mandat -
ing specific activity sequences, declarative models enable organizations to maintain pro -
cedural flexibility while ensuring compliance with essential business rules.
The Declare framework establishes a set of predefined patterns and restrictions that 
govern acceptable process behaviors, enabling organizations to handle variability and 
unexpected scenarios with greater agility (Di Ciccio et al. 2022). The field of process 
modeling has evolved to recognize that effective process representation requires con -
sideration of multiple perspectives, encompassing not only workflow sequences but also 
information flows and organizational resources. This integrated view enhances the depth 
of process understanding, facilitating more precise analysis and refinement of business 
operations (Schönig et al. 2016). Building on these principles, MP-Declare extends the 
original Declare framework (Burattin et al. 2016) to capture the intricate web of relation-
ships among various process elements, including participants, operational rules, data 
elements, and procedural flows.
The adoption of multi-perspective declarative process modeling faces a significant 
practical challenge: limited access to reference models and examples. Organizations 
typically maintain strict confidentiality around their process models since these artifacts 
can expose proprietary operational insights and competitive advantages (Feuerriegel 
et al. 2024). The academic community has established several collections of traditional 
imperative models (Corradini et al. 2019; Weske et al. 2020) and (Sola et al. 2022), yet 
comparable resources for multi-perspective declarative models remain scarce. Current 
available examples largely derive from process mining applications and are primarily 
found within specialized tools like RuM Toolkit and Declare4Py (Alman et al. 2023), 
rather than comprehensive, curated model repositories.
The emergence of artificial intelligence technologies, especially in the generative 
domain (GAI), presents novel opportunities for creating sophisticated computational 
artifacts, with notable applications in process modeling (Feuerriegel et al. 2024). These 
advanced systems demonstrate remarkable capabilities in pattern recognition and con -
tent generation, producing outputs that capture real-world complexity and variability 
with greater sophistication than conventional methodologies (Sutton 2018). Within the 
BPM domain, researchers have increasingly explored synthetic data generation, particu -
larly focusing on event log creation (Skydanienko et al. 2018; Donadello et al. 2023) and 
(Di Ciccio et al. 2015). The integration of GAI techniques with synthetic data generation 
frameworks enables researchers to create comprehensive, realistic datasets that preserve 
organizational privacy (Recker et al. 2006) and (Burattin 2015), establishing robust foun-
dations for system evaluation, experimentation, and performance assessment.
This research introduces Terpsichora, an innovative framework designed to 
address the scarcity of declarative process models through automated generation capa -
bilities. Leveraging the advanced capabilities of large language models (LLMs), our sys -
tem produces a comprehensive collection of MP-Declare models encompassing various

Preliminaries
Declarative process modeling and MP-Declare
The implementation of declarative process modeling relies fundamentally on constraint-
based specifications (Der Aalst et al. 2009). Rather than delineating sequential steps, 
this paradigm establishes a framework of rules and constraints within which process 
execution can occur dynamically. This tactic empowers process participants to exercise 
informed judgment based on situational context while maintaining adherence to critical 
business requirements and regulatory frameworks. The formal expression of these con -
straints has been systematically developed through many modeling languages such as 
Declare (Pesic et al. 2007), with MP-Declare (Burattin et al. 2016) further expanding the 
framework to encompass multiple process perspectives, including control-flow relation -
ships, data dependencies, and resource allocation considerations.
The development of declarative process modeling notations began with DecSerFlow 
in the mid-2000s and later changed the name to Declare, followed by other formal -
isms such as Dynamic Condition Response (DCR) Graphs , the Guard-Stage-Milestone 
model, and the Declarative Process Intermediate Language (DPIL) (Slaats 2020). These 
notations aimed to provide a more natural way of expressing business rules and regula -
tions while allowing for a high degree of process variability.
The adoption of declarative approaches also brought new technical challenges besides 
the need for different modeling techniques, such as the development of supporting tools 
and technologies . Furthermore, the emergence of declarative process mining techniques 
(Slaats 2020) opened up new possibilities for discovering and analyzing flexible pro -
cesses from event logs, but also introduced challenges in terms of algorithm efficiency, 
model quality, and the integration of multiple process perspectives (Burattin et al. 2016).
MP-Declare systematically incorporates four distinct types of conditions that can 
be associated with constraints: activation conditions, which specify when constraints 
become applicable based on event attributes; correlation conditions, which establish 
relationships between the attributes of activation and target events; target conditions, 
which define requirements for the target event’s attributes; and temporal conditions, 
which specify time-based relationships between events (Burattin et al. 2016). These con-
ditions are formally defined using Metric first-order temporal logic (MFOTL), enabling 
precise specification of complex business rules.
For instance, in a loan application process, MP-Declare can express sophisticated 
constraints such as “When a loan was requested and the account balance exceeds 4,000

EUR, the loan must be subsequently granted in 95% of the cases” (activation condition), 
or “When a loan is requested, it must be granted by a specific member of the finan -
cial board” (target condition). The language supports a comprehensive set of templates, 
including existence, response, precedence, and their negative counterparts, all of which 
can be enhanced with these multi-perspective conditions. This rich expressiveness 
makes MP-Declare particularly suitable for modeling complex business processes where 
decision-making depends not only on the sequence of activities but also on data values, 
resource assignments, and temporal aspects.
Large language models
LLMs have emerged as a revolutionary development in the field of Natural Language 
Processing (NLP) and GAI, fundamentally transforming computational linguistics and 
text understanding (Bommasani et al. 2022). The technological cornerstone of con -
temporary LLMs lies in the transformer architecture, whose introduction catalyzed 
the development of groundbreaking models such as BERT and GPT. This innovative 
architecture has spawned widespread industrial implementation, with major technol -
ogy companies developing their own distinctive models. Google has contributed signifi -
cantly through its BERT and T5 model implementations, while OpenAI has advanced 
the field through its iterative GPT series. Simultaneously, Meta has pursued an open-sci-
ence approach with its LLaMA model family, making advanced language models more 
accessible to the research community. In parallel, organizations like Anthropic have 
focused their efforts on addressing the ethical dimensions of AI, pioneering Constitu -
tional AI methodologies that prioritize the development of aligned and ethically con -
scious systems.
The emergence of increasingly sophisticated LLMs has given rise to prompt engineer -
ing, a specialized discipline focused on effectively communicating with and extracting 
capabilities from these models without modifying their underlying parameters. As high -
lighted by Sahoo et al. (Sahoo et al. 2024), prompt engineering has become “an indis -
pensable technique for extending the capabilities of large language models” enabling 
their seamless integration into downstream tasks through carefully crafted instructions 
known as prompts. This technique represents a paradigm shift from traditional fine-
tuning methods, as it allows practitioners to adapt pre-trained models to specific tasks 
solely through input reformulation. The significance of prompt engineering lies in its 
ability to bridge the gap between human intent and model behavior, facilitating every -
thing from basic query-response interactions to complex reasoning tasks. Rather than 
requiring expensive model retraining or extensive fine-tuning for each new application, 
prompt engineering techniques—ranging from zero-shot and few-shot prompting to 
more sophisticated approaches like chain-of-thought and tree-of-thoughts—provide a 
flexible framework for guiding model outputs.
While prompt engineering focuses on input formulation, complementary approaches 
have emerged to address output control and reliability. A notable advancement in this 
domain is the development of constrained decoding techniques, which provide deter -
ministic control over text generation processes (Tu et al. 2024). This approach has 
proven particularly valuable when LLMs need to generate outputs conforming to spe -
cific structural requirements, such as JSON schema compliance. However, the imple -
mentation of constrained decoding presents a notable trade-off: while it substantially

Despite their impressive capabilities, LLMs continue to face important challenges 
that require ongoing attention from the research community. These include concerns 
about environmental impact, bias, and fairness, as well as questions of interpretability 
and responsible governance (Bommasani et al. 2022). The development of evaluation 
frameworks and controlled generation techniques has helped address some of these 
challenges, but the field continues to evolve. The emergence of agentic workflows pres -
ents both opportunities and new challenges, including technological complexity, data 
security concerns, and the need for appropriate human oversight. As LLMs become 
increasingly integrated into various aspects of society, addressing these challenges while 
leveraging their capabilities remains crucial for ensuring their responsible development 
and deployment.
Representation theory
Representation Theory, originating from mathematics and physics, provides a frame -
work for understanding how complex systems can be described and analyzed through 
various forms of representation (Marr 1982; Weber and Lybrand 1997). In the context of 
information systems and BPM, this theory offers valuable insights into how knowledge 
is encoded, structured, and interpreted (Brachman and Levesque 1985). The core prin -
ciples of Representation Theory include:
1. Abstraction: Complex systems can be simplified through appropriate representations. 
In the real world, systems (like business processes) can be incredibly complex with 
many details. Abstraction allows us to focus on the most important aspects while 
ignoring less crucial details.
2. Isomorphism: There should be a consistent mapping between the representation and 
the represented system. This ensures that the model accurately reflects the real-world 
system. If changes happens in the model, maintainers should be able to understand 
what that would mean for the real system.
3. Inference: The representation should support reasoning about the represented 
system. This allows to use the model to understand things about the real system 
without having to directly observe or manipulate the real system every time.
In the field of BPM, Representation Theory has been applied to evaluate and improve 
process models across three key dimensions (Dumas et al. 2018):
1. Syntactic Dimension : This focuses on the formal structure of the process 
representation. It examines how well the representation adheres to the rules and 
conventions of the modeling notation used. In MP-Declare, this relates to the correct 
use of attribute types and their links with activities, declarative constraints and their 
relationships with activities, attributes and conditions. Many BP tools already provide 
features for syntactic validation, but syntactic correctness does not guarantee that the 
model is meaningful or relevant to business operations (Humm and Fengel 2012).
2. Semantic Dimension : This dimension is concerned with the meaning conveyed 
by the representation. It evaluates how accurately the model captures the intended 
business process and its underlying logic. For declarative models, this involves

assessing whether the constraints correctly express the business rules and their 
interdependencies. This aspect is more challenging because semantic consistency 
checks if the model captures the right business logic, goals, and rules. De Nicola 
et al. (De Nicola et al. 2008) have emphasized that semantic lifting, which involves 
enriching models with ontologies, can significantly reduce errors related to meaning 
and improve the alignment of business processes with organizational goals.
3. Pragmatic Dimension : This addresses the practical aspects of the representation, 
particularly its usability, effectiveness and understandability for the intended 
audience. In DBPM, this dimension is crucial for evaluating the understandability and 
communication of models, considering factors such as cognitive load and information 
processing by users (Zugal et al. 2015). Krogstie (Krogstie 2016) showed that models 
with high pragmatic quality better facilitate communication between business and IT 
stakeholders and can be more effective in process improvements.
The application of Representation Theory in BPM has led to notable insights into why 
certain process representations are more effective than others. For instance, Mendling 
et al. (Mendling et al. 2010) used the abstraction, isomorphism, and inference princi -
ples of this framework to develop quality metrics for process models, helping to quan -
tify and improve model quality across these dimensions. The Seven Process Modeling 
Guidelines (7PMG) exemplify these principles by providing a structured and empirically 
validated framework. Abstraction is addressed through the minimization and decom -
position of elements, simplifying models to focus on essential aspects and enhancing 
their understandability. Isomorphism is ensured through structured modeling and the 
use of single start and end events, promoting logical consistency and faithful representa -
tion of real-world processes. Inference is supported by avoiding ambiguous constructs 
and employing clear, verb-object activity labels, improving interpretability and enabling 
stakeholders to derive meaningful insights effectively. Together, these principles ensure 
process models are technically sound, meaningful, and practical, bridging theoretical 
concepts with real-world application.
Furthermore, the application of Representation Theory has been instrumental in eval -
uating and comparing different process modeling techniques, as demonstrated in com -
prehensive studies such as Recker et al. (Recker et al. 2009). Their analysis, utilizing the 
Bunge-Wand-Weber (BWW) representation model, revealed significant variations in 
how different techniques perform across representational dimensions. This comparative 
framework has enabled researchers to identify systematic patterns in the evolution of 
process modeling techniques, from earlier approaches like Petri nets that emphasized 
syntactic precision through minimal construct sets, to more recent techniques such 
as BPMN that provide extensive representational capabilities at the cost of increased 
complexity.
The theoretical insights derived from such analyses have profound implications for 
both research and practice in business process modeling. They highlight the inher -
ent trade-offs between completeness and clarity in representational capabilities, sug -
gesting that the selection of modeling techniques should be contingent upon specific 
organizational contexts and requirements. Moreover, these findings have informed the 
development of modeling conventions and guidelines, addressing the growing need for 
systematic approaches to manage the increasing complexity of process representations. 
This theoretical foundation has also contributed to our understanding of how different

stakeholder groups interact with process models, emphasizing the importance of bal -
ancing technical precision with pragmatic utility in process modeling initiatives.
The empirical validation of representational analyses has further illuminated the 
multifaceted nature of process modeling effectiveness (Rosemann et al. 2009). Studies 
examining practitioner interactions with various modeling techniques have revealed 
that representational deficiencies identified through theoretical analysis often manifest 
differently in practical applications, contingent upon factors such as modeler expertise, 
organizational context, and specific process domains (Recker et al. 2006; Rosemann 
and Green 2000; Rosemann et al. 2001). This nuanced understanding has led to the 
development of more sophisticated evaluation frameworks that consider not only the 
inherent representational capabilities of modeling techniques but also their contextual 
appropriateness (Green and Rosemann 2002; Davies et al. 2004) and practical utility in 
supporting business process management initiatives (Bandara et al. 2005; Bandara and 
Rosemann 2005).
In our work, we leverage Representation Theory to guide the development and evalu -
ation of synthetic data generation technique. By considering the syntactic, semantic, and 
pragmatic dimensions, we ensure that our generated data:
(1) Adheres to the formal rules of declarative process modeling (syntactic)
(2) Accurately represents realistic business processes and constraints (semantic)
(3) Produces models that are understandable and usable by stakeholders (pragmatic)
This theoretical foundation allows us to validate our synthetic data generation approach, 
ensuring that it produces high-quality, representative MP-Declare models that can effec-
tively support research and practical applications in the field.
Related work
In the field of BPM, synthetic data generation has become increasingly important for 
research and algorithm evaluation. This trend spans various aspects of BPM life-cycle, 
from event logs and process models to improvements and redesign suggestions. Below 
we group prior work by artifact and goal, then position our contribution.
Synthetic log generation
Loreti et al. (Loreti et al. 2020) introduced an innovative method for generating synthetic 
logs with both positive and negative business process traces using abductive reasoning. 
This approach addresses significant limitations in existing log generation techniques, 
particularly the lack of negative examples in synthetic datasets. The ability to generate 
both compliant and non-compliant traces is crucial for evaluating process discovery and 
conformance checking algorithms, as it allows for a more comprehensive assessment of 
their robustness and accuracy.
Li et al. (Li et al. 2022) proposed an innovative approach for generating privacy-pre -
serving process data using deep generative models. Their method, called ProcessGAN, 
addresses a critical challenge in process mining research: the inability to share confiden -
tial business process data. By leveraging generative adversarial networks (GANs), Pro -
cessGAN creates synthetic event logs that closely mimic the statistical properties and 
behavioral patterns of real-world process data while preserving privacy. The authors 
demonstrate that their approach outperforms traditional sequential models, particularly

[[page 9]]
Page 9 of 50
Santos et al. Process Science (2025) 2:21 
in generating complex, long-running process traces when trained on smaller datasets. 
ProcessGAN offers a valuable solution for researchers and practitioners, enabling the 
creation of large, diverse, and realistic event logs for process mining tasks without com -
promising sensitive business information. This work significantly contributes to the field 
by facilitating algorithm development and evaluation in scenarios where real data can -
not be shared due to privacy concerns.
In a related vein, Bergami (Bergami 2023) proposed a novel approach for fast syn -
thetic data-aware log generation tailored for temporal declarative models. This method 
addresses the challenge of efficiently generating large volumes of synthetic event logs 
that conform to complex temporal and data-aware constraints. By leveraging advanced 
graph algorithms and optimized data structures, the authors achieve significant improve-
ments in generation speed without sacrificing the quality and diversity of the produced 
logs. This work is particularly relevant for researchers and practitioners working with 
declarative process models, as it enables more efficient experimentation and evaluation 
of process mining techniques in data-aware and temporally complex scenarios.
The abductive reasoning approach presented by Loreti et al. (Loreti et al. 2020) may 
encounter computational challenges when dealing with very large or complex process 
models, potentially impacting its scalability. The method’s expressiveness is limited, as 
it may struggle to capture certain types of complex process behaviors or constraints 
that are not easily represented in the chosen logic formalism. While the approach sup -
ports both procedural and declarative models, handling declarative models, especially 
in generating negative traces, can be less intuitive and more difficult to configure due to 
the open nature of these models. Additionally, the support for data constraints requires 
direct modification of the SCIFF constraints, making the system less accessible to non-
technical users. The lack of a higher-level mechanism for handling data constraints fur -
ther complicates its application to complex data-driven processes. These limitations 
highlight the challenges in developing a comprehensive and user-friendly approach for 
generating synthetic business process traces that can accurately represent a wide range 
of process models and behaviors.
The study by Li et al. (Li et al. 2022) on ProcessGAN for generating privacy-preserving 
process data exhibits several limitations. Despite its ability to work with small datasets, 
the model still requires a minimum amount of authentic data to effectively learn process 
structures, which may be challenging in highly sensitive domains. The inherent complex-
ity of GANs leads to difficulties in optimization, with issues such as mode collapse and 
high variance in reinforcement learning rewards potentially resulting in unstable train -
ing. ProcessGAN’s sensitivity to hyperparameter settings necessitates significant effort 
for tuning across different process types. The model may struggle to accurately gener -
ate sequences for processes with high variability and complex dependencies, particularly 
when training data is limited. Furthermore, while ProcessGAN demonstrates good per -
formance with small datasets, there remains a risk of overfitting or generating biased 
sequences due to limited diversity in the training data, which could compromise the 
representativeness of the synthetic output. These limitations highlight the challenges in 
balancing privacy preservation with the generation of realistic and diverse process data.
Bergami (Bergami 2023) presents a fast approach for generating synthetic data-aware 
logs from temporal declarative models. While the method demonstrates efficiency in 
handling declarative temporal models represented by finite state automata (FSA), it has

Process models
Yan et al. (Yan et al. 2014) present a novel technique for generating synthetic collections 
of business process models that emulate the properties of real-world process reposi -
tories. Their method addresses the critical challenge of limited access to large-scale, 
diverse process model collections for research purposes. By abstracting both structural 
features (e.g., sequences, splits, joins, and loops) and semantic properties (e.g., word 
occurrence and co-occurrence in labels) from existing collections, their approach gener-
ates synthetic models that closely mirror the characteristics of real-world processes. The 
authors introduce the concept of “local maximal features” to prevent overrepresentation 
of certain structural elements, particularly sequences. Their evaluation, conducted using 
the SAP reference model comprising over 600 business process models, demonstrates 
the technique’s efficacy in producing synthetic collections that faithfully reproduce the 
statistical properties of the original dataset. This work significantly contributes to the 
field of business process management by providing researchers with a means to create 
realistic, large-scale datasets for evaluating process analysis, querying, and mining algo -
rithms, thereby enhancing the validity and generalizability of BPM research outcomes.
Camargo et al. (Camargo et al. 2021) conducted a comparative study on discover -
ing generative models from event logs, focusing on data-driven simulation versus deep 
learning approaches. This research provides valuable insights into the relative strengths 
and limitations of traditional process simulation techniques and modern deep learning 
methods in the context of process mining. By evaluating these approaches across various 
dimensions, including control-flow accuracy and temporal precision, the authors offer 
guidance on selecting appropriate generative modeling techniques based on the charac -
teristics of the process data and the specific requirements of the analysis task.
The approach proposed by Yan et al. (Yan et al. 2014) for generating synthetic process 
model collections exhibits several limitations that constrain its applicability and realism. 
Primarily, the technique is bound by the patterns and structures present in the input 
dataset, lacking the capability to generate entirely novel or innovative process structures. 
This dependency on existing patterns means that the diversity and quality of the gener -
ated models are heavily influenced by the characteristics of the initial dataset. Further -
more, while the method generates labels for process elements, it does not guarantee 
semantic coherence across the entire model, potentially resulting in unrealistic or non -
sensical process descriptions.
Additionally, the approach focuses primarily on control-flow and labels, neglecting 
other important aspects of business processes such as data flow, resources, and temporal 
dimensions. The technique’s pattern coverage is limited, potentially missing complex or 
domain-specific process patterns. There may also be discrepancies in structural proper -
ties between the generated models and real-world processes, as the method relies on

statistical abstractions that may not fully capture the nuances of actual business pro -

The comparative study by Camargo et al. (Camargo et al. 2021) highlights several limi-
tations in generating synthetic positive and negative business process traces. Deep learn-
ing approaches, while powerful, often demand substantial computational resources for 
training and generation, particularly for large-scale processes. The study reveals model-
specific limitations, with data-driven simulation methods struggling to capture complex 
parallel behaviors, while deep learning methods face challenges in precisely represent -
ing temporal aspects. Furthermore, Data-Driven Simulation (DDS) models, which rely 
on BPMN representations, inherently suffer from limited control-flow representational 
power, especially for complex synchronization constructs. The deep learning models, 
particularly LSTM-GAN, show signs of overfitting when trained on smaller logs, poten -
tially limiting their generalizability.
Process improvement
Dun et al. (Dun et al. 2023) introduce ProcessGAN, an innovative approach that lever -
ages GANs to support business process improvement (BPI). This work represents a 
significant advancement in the application of AI to the creative aspects of BPM. Pro -
cessGAN addresses the labor-intensive and time-consuming nature of traditional meth -
ods by providing computational support for generating improvement ideas. The authors 
designed ProcessGAN to learn from process execution data in the form of event logs, 
enabling it to capture and utilize positive process deviations as a source of potential 
improvements. The technique employs a GAN architecture comprising a generator and a 
discriminator, both utilizing long short-term memory (LSTM) networks. ProcessGAN’s 
training procedure incorporates novel loss functions that encourage the generation of 
process variants that deviate from undesirable patterns while maintaining feasibility. The 
authors evaluated ProcessGAN through both artificial and real-world scenarios, demon-
strating its ability to enhance the creativity of process designers, particularly in terms of 
the originality of BPI ideas.
The ProcessGAN approach proposed by Dun et al. (Dun et al. 2023) for generat -
ing business process improvement ideas through generative machine learning pres -
ents several limitations that warrant consideration. While innovative in its application 
of generative adversarial networks to business process improvement, the technique is 
primarily focused on generating improvement ideas rather than complete, executable 
process models. This focus on ideation rather than implementation means that the gen -
erated suggestions may require significant human interpretation and refinement before 
they can be applied in real-world scenarios. Furthermore, the approach’s reliance on 
existing process execution data may constrain its ability to produce truly novel or revo -
lutionary process improvements, potentially limiting its effectiveness in scenarios that 
require radical process redesign.
Another significant limitation of ProcessGAN is its narrow focus on the control-flow 
perspective of business processes. By primarily addressing the sequencing and structure 
of activities, the approach neglects other crucial aspects of process management such as 
resource allocation, data objects, and temporal constraints. This limited scope on con -
trol flow reduces the comprehensiveness of the proposed improvements and may not

an early-stage prototype, ProcessGAN’s scalability and effectiveness in complex, large-

practical applicability in diverse organizational contexts.
Summary and positioning
These studies collectively showcase the growing integration of AI and machine learn -
ing techniques in BPM, from automating model discovery and improvement suggestions 
to generating synthetic data for research and evaluation purposes. They highlight the 
potential for more efficient, accurate, and innovative approaches to process modeling, 
analysis, and improvement in the field of BPM.
In contrast to presented proposals and methods that require extensive model training 
or heavy domain-specific scripting, our LLM-based approach leverages prompt-based 
mechanisms to create new process models without the need for costly fine-tuning on 
large datasets. This design choice lowers computational overhead and reduces entry bar-
riers, empowering both technical and non-technical stakeholders to incorporate new 
constraints or desired behaviors—such as multi-level compliance rules, dynamic con -
currency thresholds, or semantic context cues—simply by specifying them in natural 
language.
Additionally, while many existing approaches focus on control flow to the detriment of 
resource usage, temporal dependencies, or data objects, our solution explicitly factors in 
these dimensions, fostering a richer and more realistic representation of complex busi -
ness processes. Finally, by incorporating automated validation loops, we mitigate issues 
such as mode collapse, overfitting, or biased sequence generation, thereby enhancing 
the robustness, diversity, and real-world fidelity of the synthetic models. Collectively, 
these design choices position our framework as a comprehensive solution that not only 
advances the state of synthetic business process data generation but also lays a founda -
tion for future innovations in process improvement and analytics.

Prompt engineering techniques
To interplay with LLMs, we created a metamodel (Fig. 2) representing the structural 
definition of an MP-Declare model (Schönig et al. 2016) and (Burattin et al. 2016). This 
metamodel was used to specify the constructs involved in MP-Declare in the form of 
entities and their relationships. This metamodel was converted to a data model using 
Pydantic4, a data validation library for Python. Below, we explain the implementation of 
each construct in the metamodel, detailing the goal of each class, its rules, and attributes.
The Activity class represents an activity within a process model. It is characterized 
by its name (a string), and description (which provides a detailed explanation of the 
activity’s purpose). The Attribute class defines attributes of a process, characterized 
by its type (which indicates whether the attribute is an integer, float, or enumeration), 
name (specifying the attribute’s name) and description (providing a detailed expla -
nation of the attribute); optionally, min_value and max_value fields define the range 
for integer or float attributes, while enumeration_values is a list of possible values 
for enumeration attributes.
The Bind class represents the linkage between activities and their attributes in a 
model. Hence, each bind is defined by an Activity (which represents the Activ-
ity involved in the binding), attributes (a list of Attribute objects bound to 
the activity), and a description (providing a description of the bind’s purpose). The 
Constraint class models the constraints governing process execution, characterized 
by its type (specifying if the constraint is unary or binary), description (a detailed 
explanation of the constraint), template (which specifies the constraint’s template), 
activation (representing the activation activity, required for both unary and binary

The MPDeclareModel is the main class and encapsulates the entire MP-Declare

activities, binds, attributes, and constraints into a coherent format suitable for parsing to 
Declare4Py.
The application of role-play prompting was implemented by specifying in a prompt 
that the assistant is an expert in declarative process modeling and analysis. The primary 
objective of this technique aligns with recent findings that LLMs can effectively embody 
specialized roles when given detailed agent descriptions (Xu et al. 2023; Shanahan et 
al. 2023). As demonstrated in ExpertPrompting, providing customized expert identi -
ties improves response quality and specificity - leading to up to 48.5% preference rate in 
GPT-4 based evaluations compared to 23% for standard prompting (Xu et al. 2023). This 
approach is further supported by Wang et al. (Wang et al. 2024), who demonstrate that 
LLMs can accurately reproduce the personalities of target characters, achieving align -
ment scores of up to 80.7% when properly prompted with role-specific instructions.
Through this approach, we aim to enhance the declarative modeling outcomes by 
leveraging the LLMs ability to maintain consistent expert personas. This is particularly 
relevant as Shanahan et al. (Shanahan et al. 2023) note that LLMs can effectively simu -
late a “superposition of simulacra” - maintaining coherent expert behaviors when prop -
erly prompted. Their research, along with findings from Chen et al. (Chen et al. 2024), 
shows that while LLMs do not literally embody expertise, they can reliably role-play 
domain specialists in their responses when given appropriate context. The responses 
tend to be more comprehensive and technically precise - for example, ExpertPrompt -
ing produced answers that were on average 27.5% longer and more detailed than stan -
dard prompts (Xu et al. 2023). Moreover, Chen et al. (Chen et al. 2024) demonstrates 
that LLMs can effectively respond to explicit constraints in instructions, with top com -
mercial LLMs achieving high accuracy across various control tasks including sentiment, 
topic, and multi-aspect control.
Knowledge injection was implemented to address hallucinations observed in earlier 
iterations where the LLM generated incorrect or inconsistent interpretations of MP-
Declare constructs . The technique involves providing the LLM with explicit knowledge 
about MP-Declare elements, their semantics, and valid relationships between compo -
nents. This approach aligns with findings from Martino et al. (Martino et al. 2023), who 
demonstrate that knowledge injection can significantly reduce hallucination in LLM 
outputs while improving the overall quality of generated content. Their experiments 
showed that models augmented with injected knowledge produced 205% more correct 
assertions while reducing incorrect statements by 18%, even enabling smaller models to 
outperform larger ones in terms of output quality and factual accuracy.

The effectiveness of knowledge injection for process-related tasks is further supported 
by recent research in the field. Kourani et al. (Kourani et al. 2024, 2024) demonstrate its 
value in process modeling and comprehension tasks, where injected technical knowl -
edge helps maintain model consistency and adherence to formal specifications. This 
aligns with findings from Berti (Berti 2024), who show that knowledge injection can help 
LLMs maintain technical precision when dealing with process mining concepts. More -
over, Fu et al. (Fu et al. 2023) emphasize the importance of selective knowledge injection, 
demonstrating that carefully curated knowledge leads to better outcomes than injecting 
large volumes of potentially irrelevant information.
Few-shot learning was implemented to demonstrate examples of valid MP-Declare 
structures that were previously injected through knowledge injection. This included 
examples of activities, attributes, bindings between activities and attributes, constraint 
specifications, and condition specifications. The technique builds upon the foundational 
work of Brown et al. (Brown et al. 2020), who first demonstrated that LLMs can learn 
to perform tasks from just a few examples without any gradient updates or fine-tuning. 
Their research showed that few-shot learning enables models to adapt to new tasks by 
conditioning on a small number of demonstrations within the model’s context window. 
This is particularly relevant for declarative process modeling, where complex structural 
patterns and relationships need to be accurately captured and reproduced.
The effectiveness of few-shot learning in process-related tasks has been demonstrated 
across several recent studies. Vidgof et al. (Vidgof et al. 2023) envisioned few-shot tech -
niques to business process management tasks, showing that providing examples of valid 
process structures could significantly improve the quality and consistency of generated 
outputs. Similarly, Busch et al. (Busch et al. 2023) found that few-shot prompting led 
to more accurate and contextually appropriate responses in process management sce -
narios compared to zero-shot approaches. Rizk et al. (Rizk et al. 2023) argued for the 
development of process-specific foundation models, highlighting how few-shot learning 
could help address the challenges of data scarcity and domain-specific language in BPM 
tasks. This aligns with findings from Ayad and AlSayoud (Ayad and AlSayoud 2024), who 
demonstrated that few-shot prompting, when combined with chain-of-thought reason -
ing, produced more refined and domain-specific suggestions for business process model 
improvements compared to zero-shot approaches, though generating fewer but more 
precise activities.
The final prompt design integrated these three techniques - role-play prompting, 
knowledge injection, and few-shot learning. First, the prompt established the LLMs role 
as a declarative process modeling expert, setting the groundwork for domain-specific 
responses. Then, it incorporated the injected knowledge about MP-Declare constructs, 
including their formal definitions, valid relationships, and semantic rules. Finally, the 
prompt included carefully selected examples demonstrating proper MP-Declare struc -
tures, leveraging few-shot learning to guide the model toward generating valid and 
contextually appropriate responses. This combined approach aimed to maximize the 
benefits of each technique: the domain expertise simulation from role-play prompting, 
the technical precision from knowledge injection, and the structural guidance from few-
shot learning. The integration of these techniques was designed to produce responses 
that are both technically accurate and semantically meaningful within the context of 
declarative process modeling given by the user.

Conclusion
This research contributes both to the theoretical understanding and practical applica -
tion of LLMs in declarative process modeling, particularly in addressing the critical chal-
lenge of model availability in DBPM. Our work advances three key areas identified in the 
emerging field of synthetic process model generation: prompt engineering for declara -
tive constraints, multi-perspective model generation, and automated validation of gen -
erated artifacts.
In the realm of prompt engineering for declarative constraints, we introduce novel 
approaches for bridging the gap between natural language instructions and formal MP-
Declare specifications. Our framework demonstrates how carefully crafted prompting 
strategies combining role-play, knowledge injection, and few-shot learning can effec -
tively guide LLMs to generate syntactically valid and semantically meaningful process 
models. This contribution is particularly significant as it shows how LLMs can serve as 
reliable generators of complex declarative constraints while maintaining domain rel -
evance and structural consistency.
Regarding multi-perspective model generation, our research advances the state-of-
the-art in synthetic data generation by introducing two distinct yet complementary 
approaches - Function Calling and Structured Output. These methods demonstrate how 
LLMs can systematically generate not only control-flow constraints but also data attri -
butes, resource assignments, and temporal conditions, addressing a fundamental limita -
tion in current synthetic model generation approaches. This contribution is especially 
valuable for researchers and practitioners seeking diverse, realistic process models for 
evaluation and experimentation while maintaining privacy and confidentiality require -
ments. In terms of automated validation, we contribute new methods for ensuring the 
quality and reliability of generated models through systematic verification of syntactic

correctness, semantic coherence, and pragmatic utility. Our approach demonstrates how 
automated validation can facilitate the creation of large-scale, diverse model collections 
while maintaining consistent quality standards across different business domains and 
complexity levels.
Specific technical contributions
The centerpiece of our work is Terpsichora’s metamodel-based implementation that 
provides a robust technical foundation for LLM-based process model generation. The 
framework’s core innovation lies in its hierarchical validation architecture: a Pydantic-
based metamodel implementation that enforces MP-Declare specifications through sys -
tematic type checking and relationship validation; an intermediate validation layer that 
ensures semantic coherence through automated constraint verification; and a final vali -
dation stage that confirms pragmatic utility through process mining techniques.
This multi-layered approach achieved remarkable precision in structural generation, 
with quantitative metrics demonstrating high consistency across model sizes (den -
sity metrics maintaining 0.822–0.888 across batches) and strong adherence to labeling 
conventions (98.5% compliance rate). The framework’s implementation of controlled 
temperature settings (0) and structured feedback loops for regeneration ensures deter -
ministic outputs while maintaining generation quality. These technical components 
work in concert through a modular architecture that enables systematic generation of 
MP-Declare models while allowing for future enhancements and adaptations to specific 
generation requirements.
Practical and theoretical implications
Our work through Terpsichora extends beyond immediate technical contributions 
to offer broader implications for research methodology and industry practice in pro -
cess modeling. From a theoretical perspective, our results provide empirical validation 
of Representation Theory in automated model generation. The framework’s success in 
maintaining consistent structural metrics while accommodating domain-specific varia -
tions demonstrates how automated generation can balance formal requirements with 
representativity. This finding contributes to ongoing theoretical discussions about the 
relationship between model complexity and understandability in declarative process 
management.
For practitioners, Terpsichora transforms the landscape of process model availability 
by enabling on-demand generation of contextually relevant examples. This capability 
addresses a long-standing challenge in process mining research and education, where 
access to diverse, realistic process models has been limited by confidentiality concerns. 
Organizations can now generate domain-specific training materials and test cases with -
out compromising sensitive business data, while researchers gain access to larger, more 
diverse datasets for empirical studies. The framework’s demonstrated ability to maintain 
consistent quality across different complexity levels (evidenced by stable density met -
rics of 0.822–0.888) ensures that generated models serve as reliable references for both 
learning and analysis.

Limitations and threats to validity
Our research with Terpsichora, while demonstrating promising results in synthetic MP-
Declare model generation, faces several important limitations and validity concerns that 
warrant careful consideration. A fundamental limitation stems from the current capa -
bilities of LLMs in handling complex constraint combinations. While our framework 
achieves high accuracy in basic constraint generation, we observed increased variability 
in handling complex negative constraints and intricate temporal conditions. This limita -
tion becomes particularly evident in models requiring sophisticated interplay between 
activation conditions and correlation conditions, where the framework sometimes gen -
erates overly simplified constraint patterns.
From a methodological perspective, our evaluation framework encounters challenges 
related to both internal and external validity. The absence of a comprehensive bench -
mark dataset for MP-Declare models makes it difficult to definitively assess the quality 
and representativeness of our generated models against real-world examples. While our 
metrics demonstrate strong internal consistency, the validation relies heavily on struc -
tural characteristics and may not fully capture the nuanced semantic relationships found 
in actual business processes. To mitigate these concerns, we implemented a multi-fac -
eted validation approach combining automated metrics with process mining validation, 
though we acknowledge that this may not completely address the challenge of semantic 
validation.
The practical application of Terpsichora faces several technical constraints. First, the 
framework’s reliance on GPT-4s API introduces both cost considerations and potential 
access limitations for large-scale model generation. Second, while our approach dem -
onstrates robust performance in controlled settings, its effectiveness may vary across 
different business domains, particularly those with specialized terminology or complex 
regulatory requirements. The framework’s current implementation also shows limita -
tions in handling very large models, as evidenced by increased variability in constraint 
distributions for models with higher complexity levels. Additionally, the generation pro -
cess can sometimes produce redundant or conflicting constraints when dealing with 
extensive multi-perspective requirements, necessitating additional validation cycles.
To strengthen the validity of our findings, we implemented several mitigation strate -
gies. These include systematic documentation of all generation parameters, cross-val -
idation through both FC and SO methods, and empirical validation through process 
rediscovery. However, we acknowledge inherent limitations in our current approach. 
The rapid evolution of LLM capabilities means that our findings are specifically tied to 
GPT-4s current capabilities, and results may vary with different models or future itera -
tions. Furthermore, while our framework demonstrates strong performance in generat -
ing syntactically valid models, ensuring deep semantic correspondence with real-world 
processes remains a challenging open problem. These limitations and validity concerns 
provide important context for interpreting our results and highlight crucial areas for 
future research in LLM-based process model generation.
