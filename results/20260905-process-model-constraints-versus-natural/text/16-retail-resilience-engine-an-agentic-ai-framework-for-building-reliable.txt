ABSTRACT System reliability and operational resilience are two critical success factors in the retail
industry that are directly connected to customer satisfaction and business sustainability. Staying competitive
in today’s dynamic and rapidly evolving market requires rapid adaptability. However, it contradicts the
reliability and resilience. This paper proposes an innovative solution, the Retail Resilience Engine (RRE),
to establish a balance between these success factors and market demand. It is a unique framework that
combines Test-Driven Development (TDD) with a Large Language Model (LLM). This framework follows
the state-of-the-art Agentic-AI architecture. It effectively evaluates the decision-making process at rapid
speed in retail by incorporating diverse factors, including inventory management, demand forecasting, and
customer feedback. As a result, the system reliability is improved significantly. The experimental analysis
of the proposed framework shows its decision-making is similar to human experts with a similarity index of
97.5%. It further proves the reliability of the system. The framework also scales effectively, maintaining high
accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 scores across varying dataset sizes. The robustness analysis of the system
demonstrates the agility enhancement across diverse retail domains, ensuring consistent performance with
accuracy exceeding 90% across all tested scenarios. The integration of a creative filtering mechanism further
enhances the performance of the RRE framework by preventing 98.2% of the irrelevant inputs. Overall, the
proposed RRE framework demonstrates the impressive potential to transform retail systems by enhancing
reliability, scalability, and decision-making quality through an Agentic-AI approach.

Dynamic scaling with consumer demands while maintaining
sustainable business growth are two success factors in
retail business [1]. According to Pasch et al. [2], customer
retention and system reliability are intertwined in the retail
industry. That is why system resilience plays a crucial
role in this sector. A reliable and resilient retail system
ensures that it can minimize the number of unpredictable

C. AIM OF RESEARCH
The recent advancement in agentic AI backed by Large
Language Models (LLMs) has made it possible to analyze
and correlate numerous interdependent elements of the
retail industry [9]. However, while TDD and AI-driven
models have shown promise individually, their integration
remains underexplored in retail system design. This lack
of integration results in systems that may either focus
on structural testing without real-time adaptability or rely
heavily on data analytics without strong functional testing
foundations. This paper proposes the Retail Resilience
Engine (RRE), an agentic AI framework integrated with
TDD. The primary objectives of this research are as
follows:
• Agentic AI Framework with TDD Integration:
Design a framework that combines TDD principles
with agentic AI that facilitates rapid development and
minimizes the probability of system unavailability or
unintentional performance issues.
• Enhance Scalability and Robustness: Ensuring that
the framework can scale according to the demand
and maintains acceptable performance for different
scenarios.
• Feasibility Study: Feasibility analysis of replacing
human specialists in retail responsible for TDD through
multiple experiments.
D. CONTRIBUTIONS AND SIGNIFICANCE
The RRE uses a Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT)
as the agent and, with a creative agentic AI framework archi-
tecture, gains the capability of processing both structured
and unstructured data. It is one of the primary approaches
of replicating the TDD approach in retail with LLM as a
replacement for a human specialist. The core contributions
of this study are listed as follows:
• Conceptualization of the TDD integration with LLM to
introduce an alternative to a human specialist in the retail
industry.
• Design and implementation of the agentic AI framework
with a GPT as the agent and introducing the retail
resilience engine.
• Analysis of the feasibility of the proposed system
through multiple relevant experiment to discover the
practicality of the solution.

A. SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT PRACTICES IN RETAIL
SYSTEMS
The retail industry relies heavily on well-defined software
development practices to ensure operational reliability and
customer satisfaction. Traditional approaches, such as Agile
and Waterfall methodologies, have been pivotal for building
foundational retail systems [12]. However, their ability to
adapt to rapidly changing market dynamics remains lim-
ited [13]. Test-Driven Development (TDD) has emerged as
a software development paradigm emphasizing the creation
of test cases before code implementation. TDD ensures
that retail systems are developed with minimal errors and
greater reliability [14]. However, as Koller et al. [8] high-
light, traditional TDD struggles with dynamic environments
where multiple interdependent elements must be handled
simultaneously.
B. AI AND NLP ADVANCEMENTS IN RETAIL SYSTEMS
The advancement of Artificial Intelligence (AI), particularly
Natural Language Processing (NLP)-based systems, has
shown significant promise in retail decision support systems.
AI models can process large volumes of structured and
unstructured data, assisting with demand forecasting, cus-
tomer sentiment analysis, and inventory management [15].
A systematic review by Anica-Popa et al. [16] identified
the transformative potential of AI in retail systems but
also highlighted challenges related to scalability and data
reliability. Heins and Caroline [17] provided further insights
into the role of AI in demand forecasting, emphasizing the
need for real-time decision support systems. Despite these
advancements, most retail AI applications remain standalone
solutions with limited integration into software development
methodologies such as TDD. This creates a gap where
AI-driven insights remain separate from the core software
engineering processes.
C. COMBINING TDD AND AI FOR RETAIL RESILIENCE
The combination of TDD with LLM complements each
other and help producing reliable and rapidly adaptable retail
systems. A study conducted by Mathews and Nagappan [18]
and Piya and Sullivan [19] explored the combination of
TDD principles with large language models (LLMs). The
aim of this study was code generation and validation.
Although it doesn’t directly align with the retail industry,
it has overlapping features. Their results highlighted faster
development cycles and improved error detection compared
to traditional methods. Despite the noticeable potential, the
investigation by Runsewe et al. [20] pointed out that the
development of such a framework integrating both TDD
and LLM is an under-explored field of research. As a
matter of fact, the agentic AI framework concept for the
retail industry is absent from most of the state-of-the-art
literature. Most of the papers analyzed in this literature
review indicate that the primary focus of these studies is on
proof-of-concept rather than practical implementation. The
proposed Retail Resilience Engine (RRE) aims to bridge
this gap by integrating TDD principles with a generative
AI-powered system. The goal is to make it capable of
handling large-scale retail datasets while ensuring system
reliability and adaptability. Recent advancements in image
processing and saliency detection have demonstrated signifi-
cant potential in improving AI-driven retail systems. Studies
such as [21], [22] introduce methods for enhancing feature
extraction, relevance assessment, and anomaly detection,
which can strengthen the decision-making capabilities of AI
models. The integration of these saliency-based techniques
in retail systems could be beneficial for tasks such as
inventory quality assessment, detecting misplaced products,
and identifying defective goods in real-time [23], [24]. While
RRE primarily focuses on NLP-based decision-making,
incorporating such methodologies in future iterations could
further enhance system reliability and operational efficiency.
D. AI-BASED TEST AUTOMATION IN COMMERCIAL
SOFTWARE ENGINEERING
Recent advancements in AI-driven test automation have
significantly improved the efficiency and accuracy of soft-
ware development processes, particularly in commercial
applications. AI models are now widely employed for
automated test case generation, bug prediction, and software
maintenance [25], [26]. A study by Garg et al. [25] explores
the role of AI in optimizing software testing workflows,
demonstrating that deep learning-based automated test case
generation reduces defect rates by 27% in commercial
software projects. Similarly, Kumar and Bhardwaj [26] intro-
duce a hybrid AI framework that integrates NLP-based test
generation with reinforcement learning for self-improving
test automation, leading to a 33% improvement in code cov-
erage for enterprise software. Despite these advancements,
existing literature primarily focuses on static AI-driven
automation tools that do not integrate real-time adaptability.
The proposed Retail Resilience Engine (RRE) extends this
research by embedding TDD principles into an Agentic
AI-driven framework, allowing for continuous learning and
automated test evolution based on dynamic retail system

III. METHODOLOGY
Both reliability and adaptability are key features essential for
today’s retail system [27]. The methodology of the proposed
retail resilience engine has been developed to achieve both
of these while maintaining a proper balance. It has been
made possible because of the integration of Test-Driven
Development (TDD) principles [28] with Natural Lan-
guage Processing (NLP)-based Artificial Intelligence (AI).
A conceptual explanation of the complete sequential method-
ological development process is presented in this section.
A. RRE AGENTIC AI FRAMEWORK
The overview of the proposed RRE agentic AI framework
is illustrated in Figure 1. It shows the high-level modules
abstracting the low-level functionalities. The framework has
been developed using a modular structure. This structure
introduces structural scalability of the framework where
additional modules can be integrated when necessary. The
framework receives data from diverse sources. The data
sources include both structured and unstructured data. Rele-
vant data are extracted from the source, cleaned, tokenized,
and properly split for the next phase. The next phase is
feature management, where the features are permanently

transformer block maintains communication with the rest of
the framework with agentic AI API. It is controlled by two

according to the command received from the user interface.
The command from the user interface is transferred to the
agent through the application and business logic layer.

6) SPLITTING FOR GENERALIZATION
To ensure robust generalization, the dataset was divided into
80% training, 10% validation, and 10% testing sets. The
split was performed using a stratified sampling approach to
maintain the distribution of different types of retail queries
across all subsets. This ensures that the model learns from
diverse retail-related data without overfitting to any particular
scenario. To further evaluate generalization, instances in the
test set were strictly chosen from previously unseen retail
cases. That is, the testing dataset contained retail scenarios
that were not present in the training set, simulating real-world
deployment conditions where the system must generalize
to new inputs. Additionally, during the training phase, the
validation set was used for hyperparameter tuning and early
stopping, preventing overfitting to the training data. This
systematic dataset split ensures that the Retail Resilience
Engine can make accurate predictions on unseen retail data,
demonstrating its adaptability and real-world applicability.

A key distinguishing factor of the Retail Resilience Engine
(RRE) is its direct integration of Test-Driven Development
(TDD) principles into its workflow, rather than merely relying
on TDD-based data. The system incorporates automatically
generated test cases derived from structured specifications
and requirements, ensuring that every system modification
adheres to predefined test conditions before implementation.
These test cases are generated dynamically, evaluated against
expected outputs, and stored as a continuously evolving
validation set.
Furthermore, TDD-specific data in the RRE differ from
conventional retail system data in several ways. Instead
of only containing logs of system execution or business
transactions, TDD data include test scripts, pass/fail logs,
bug reports, and structured feedback from automated test
suites. This structured approach ensures that system updates
follow a write-test-then-develop cycle, reinforcing reliability
and reducing errors before deployment. By embedding TDD
principles within both the training dataset and the operational
pipeline, the RRE enforces a rigorous testing-driven refine-
ment process that enhances decision-making reliability.

V. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE SCOPE
While the Retail Resilience Engine demonstrates robust
performance and significant potential, there are certain lim-
itations that present opportunities for future improvements.
These limitations and their corresponding future research
directions are discussed below.
A. ETHICAL AND DATA PRIVACY CHALLENGES
The Retail Resilience Engine relies on sensitive retail data,
including sales records and customer feedback. This raises
concerns about data privacy and ethical use, particularly when
handling personally identifiable information (PII). Ensuring
compliance with data protection regulations such as GDPR
and CCPA is essential for broader adoption. Future work
will focus on incorporating advanced privacy-preserving
techniques, such as federated learning and differential
privacy, to mitigate these concerns.
B. DOMAIN-SPECIFIC CONSTRAINTS
While the engine performs well across various retail domains,
its effectiveness is currently limited to predefined categories
such as inventory management and demand forecasting.
Expanding its applicability to niche retail sectors, such as
luxury goods or localized markets, will require additional
domain-specific training data and model fine-tuning.
C. EXPLAINABILITY AND TRANSPARENCY
The engine operates as a black-box system, making it difficult
for users to understand the rationale behind its recommen-
dations. This lack of explainability can hinder trust and
adoption among retail professionals. Future iterations will
incorporate explainable AI (XAI) techniques to provide clear
insights into decision-making processes, thereby enhancing
user confidence.
D. SCALABILITY TO REAL-TIME APPLICATIONS
Although the engine scales well with increased dataset
sizes, its ability to process queries in real-time retail
environments requires further optimization. Enhancing the
system’s computational efficiency through techniques such
as model compression and hardware acceleration will enable
real-time decision-making capabilities.
E. PARAMETER OPTIMIZATION
The engine’s performance relies heavily on hyperparameter
configurations, which were set based on heuristic meth-
ods [55]. A systematic exploration of optimal parameter
settings through advanced optimization techniques, such
as Bayesian optimization, will likely improve the engine’s
overall accuracy and efficiency.
F. INTEGRATION WITH IoT AND EDGE DEVICES
Currently, the engine is designed for centralized processing in
cloud environments. Integrating it with the Internet of Things
(IoT) and edge computing devices will enable decentralized
decision-making and reduce latency, which is critical for
real-time retail operations [56]. Future research will explore
hybrid cloud-edge architectures to address this limitation.
G. SALIENCY-BASED ANOMAL Y DETECTION
In addition to leveraging NLP and structured data process-
ing, the potential incorporation of saliency-based anomaly

H. REDUCING HUMAN EFFORT
Reducing human effort in software deployment remains a
critical challenge in modern system automation. While the
Retail Resilience Engine significantly reduces dependency
on manual intervention through the integration of TDD
and LLM-based decision-making, achieving full automa-
tion in complex retail environments still requires further
refinements. Future research will explore reinforcement
learning techniques and adaptive automation strategies to
enhance RRE’s self-learning capability, minimizing human
intervention in the iterative development process.
I. CHALLENGES OF AGENTIC AI IN DYNAMIC RETAIL
ENVIRONMENTS
While Agentic AI enhances decision-making through
dynamic adaptability, it presents specific challenges in
fast-changing retail environments. One primary limitation
is decision latency—the time required for AI-driven agents
to analyze complex scenarios and make optimal decisions.
In high-frequency retail operations, such as flash sales or
supply chain disruptions, even minor delays in AI-driven
responses may impact overall efficiency.
Additionally, handling unpredictable retail scenarios
remains a challenge. While the model is trained on extensive
datasets, it may still struggle with unprecedented market
conditions, such as emerging consumer trends, supply
chain bottlenecks, or economic fluctuations, that are not
well-represented in the training data. Ensuring adaptability
requires continuous model retraining and real-time learning
mechanisms.
Lastly, the reliance on high-quality data introduces poten-
tial risks. If incoming data is incomplete, biased, or incon-
sistent, the Agentic AI framework may generate suboptimal
or unreliable recommendations. Future improvements will
focus on enhanced anomaly detection mechanisms and
reinforcement learning techniques to enable better real-time
adaptation in dynamic retail settings.
The limitations discussed above serve as a roadmap for
enhancing the Retail Resilience Engine. Addressing these
challenges will further improve its practicality, reliability, and
adoption across diverse retail scenarios.
VI. CONCLUSION
The Retail Resilience Engine (RRE) is a novel framework
designed to address the growing challenges in retail sys-
tems, such as inventory optimization, customer sentiment
analysis, and demand forecasting. By integrating Test-Driven
Development (TDD) with Agentic AI, the engine ensures
rapid adaptability while maintaining system reliability and
scalability.
This study successfully achieved all research objec-
tives. The feasibility of integrating TDD with Large
Language Models (LLMs) was demonstrated through
a structured methodology and experimental validation.
The system was designed to support automated test
generation, reducing reliance on human-driven software
validation. Moreover, the RRE framework exhibited
high scalability and robustness across diverse retail
environments, meeting the goal of enhancing system
adaptability.
A key focus of this study was to analyze the feasibility
of replacing human specialists responsible for TDD in
retail system development. The experimental results from
the Retail Decision Impact Alignment (RDIA) experiment
demonstrated that the system’s decision-making closely
aligned with human expert evaluations, achieving a similarity
index of 97.5%. Additionally, the implementation plan
quality assessment showed that AI-generated TDD plans
were on par with expert-designed strategies. These findings
indicate that AI-driven TDD frameworks can significantly
reduce human intervention while maintaining software reli-
ability, supporting the feasibility of automated retail system
development.
Despite its promising performance, the study acknowl-
edges certain limitations, including potential biases in
training data, explainability challenges, and real-time scala-
bility concerns. Addressing these limitations through future
research will further improve the adoption of AI-driven TDD
frameworks.
In conclusion, this work presents a practical and scalable
solution for AI-driven TDD in retail systems, demonstrating
that automated approaches can replicate human expertise
in system validation and decision-making. By reducing
dependency on human specialists and ensuring continuous
improvement through structured testing, the proposed RRE
framework represents a significant step forward in developing
reliable and intelligent retail systems.
